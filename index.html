A tiny guide to better messages

Urgent should mean urgent.

If it can wait for someone to finish what they’re doing, it probably doesn’t need the urgent label.

The simple rule

Urgency is about impact, not impatience.

Use an urgent tag when someone needs to act now to prevent a real problem. If the task can wait until they’re available, send it normally and include when you actually need it.

Same ask. Better signal.

A clear deadline helps people prioritize without making every message feel like an interruption.

× Not urgent

Bob
URGENT — Can you review the draft today? Whenever you get a chance.

The “urgent” label conflicts with “whenever you get a chance.”

✓ Clear and considerate

Maria
Could you review the draft by Thursday afternoon? I’m sending it Friday morning. No need to stop what you’re doing.

A real deadline and a little context make the priority clear.

If it really is urgent…

Say what happened, what you need, and by when. A little context helps someone act quickly.

URGENT: [what happened]. Please [action] by [time] because [impact]. I’ve already [steps taken].
